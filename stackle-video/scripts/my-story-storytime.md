# My Story, StoryTime: Engineer to Founder, Teacher and YouTuber: Script

**Draft 1. About 550 spoken words are written. Your answers fill about 25 slots. At two or three sentences each, they add about 800 to 1,000 words, for about 1,350 to 1,550 words, or 9 to 10 minutes at 150 words a minute. A longer Chapter 5 is the best place to add time.**

How to read this draft:

- **[FILL: ...]** marks a part only you can tell. The script rules say never to invent a scene, a struggle or a quote, so each one has a question and a short frame you can say it in. Answer in plain notes and I will turn them into lines.
- **[CHECK: ...]** marks something I took from your notes, or a line I suggested, that needs a yes before you film it.
- Everything else uses only facts from your notes: the stackle-linkedin-posts skill, the content plan and the asks README.

## How this video is different from a normal Stackle script

1. **The intro is one line.** The whole video is the intro, so the standard four-fact intro would repeat the story. The 12-second channel intro clip is left out for the same reason.
2. **It is mostly on camera.** In a story, your face carries the video. About a third of it is pictures: your real photos, one timeline that comes back between chapters, and two cards. A normal Stackle script is about half graphics.
3. **Some chapters end with one line for the viewer.** These are the chapters where viewers are today: student, first job, new country, a failed interview. Each line is one thing to do, not a lesson. This keeps a personal story useful on a career channel, and each line can become a Short.

## How to make the story land when you film it

1. **Film the cold open last.** By the end of the session you'll be warm, and the first 15 seconds decide whether people stay.
2. **Slow down in Chapter 5.** Speak slower, leave longer pauses, and have the editor punch in closer. The low point is the part people remember.
3. **End each chapter on the next one.** The last line of a chapter should make the viewer want the next. For example, end Chapter 4 on "And that's when I got my first big tech interview."
4. **Use real photos.** Even a blurry phone photo of your first desk works better than stock footage, because the viewer knows it is real.
5. **Let the timeline do the work.** It comes back five times and fills in a little more each time, so the viewer always knows where they are in the story. It can use the same hand-drawn page style as your channel intro (`source/kit`).
6. **Keep the money number if you can.** The reference video is built around one number. If you share your first salary in Chapter 3, consider putting it in the title.
