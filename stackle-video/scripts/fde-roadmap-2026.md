### What all of them cover (our script covers this too)

- **The definition:** an engineer who works with customers after the sale and writes production code for them.
- **The origin:** Palantir.
- **Who is hiring:** OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI companies building FDE teams.
- **A skills list:**
  - Python and APIs
  - cloud and DevOps
  - LLMs, RAG and agents
  - customer communication
- **Portfolio projects,** usually a RAG app and an agent.
- **Salary,** often as the main hook.

### Where they are wrong, out of date, or disagree

- **"$1M job" framing (videos 5 and 6).** A small number of senior roles at top AI labs can reach that range, mostly in stock, so it is not typical pay. Typical pay:
  - The median advertised FDE salary is more than $188,000 (Lightcast, via Fortune).
  - The Levels.fyi median total pay is $216,250.
- **Microsoft "6,000 FDE hires" (video 1, Telegram post).** Microsoft's Frontier Co. pools about 6,000 existing staff: FDEs, consultants, industry specialists and salespeople. It is not 6,000 new FDE hires. **The script leaves this out.**
- **Timelines disagree.** Video 7 promises 90 days, video 3 promises 6 months, and written guides say 12 to 18 months for new graduates. What the job posts actually show: only 12% of FDE postings ask for 0 to 2 years of experience, and 60% ask for 3 to 5 years (Bloomberry). The script says this plainly.
- **Growth numbers get mixed up.** Each of these uses a different dataset and period, so never combine them:
  - "800%" is the FT and Indeed figure for Jan to Sep 2025.
  - "729%" is Indeed via Business Insider, April 2025 to April 2026.
  - "1,165%" is Bloomberry, 2025 vs 2024.
  - "More than 1,000%" is Lightcast via Fortune, Jan to Aug 2026 vs 2025.
  - "5,000%" and "5,230%" figures also circulate, but their baseline is garbled. Avoid them.
- **Stale call to action (video 3).** Its bootcamp started Oct 4, 2026.

### Gaps, and the angle this script takes

1. **Every number has a source,** shown on screen and listed in the description. This answers the $1M hype directly.
2. **The job is defined by the customer's result, not by a tool list.** None of the roadmap videos frame the job this way.
3. **Palantir's Dev and Delta split is explained in one line.** The other videos mention Palantir but do not explain it.
4. **The experience question gets an honest answer.** The FDE role is usually a second or third job, and a 90-day plan only works if you already have engineering experience.
5. **The roadmap puts data before AI.** Data and cloud come before applied AI. This is your data engineering angle, and it matches what job posts and the MIT study say about where projects fail.
6. **Projects are built like real deployments.** Each one has a real user, is put online, has an eval score and ends with a one-page case study.
7. **The interview section includes your hiring experience.** It also warns about jobs that use the FDE title for consulting work.

That's because a company wants its problem solved this quarter, not a research project. **[PAUSE]**

> The last skill in this phase is evals, which just means test questions with known answers that you run after every change. A lot of evals means you can prove your system got better, and a little means you're guessing. Let's say you change one line in a prompt and run your hundred test questions. If the score goes up, you can ship that change with proof, because customers trust a score more than a demo.

**[PAUSE]**

Discovery just means asking those questions. A lot of discovery means you build the right thing once, and a little means you build the wrong thing twice. Let's say a manager asks you for a chatbot. A few questions later, you learn the staff just can't find the right form, which means a simple search might fix it faster.

> Number five is proof of work, which means you build projects the way an FDE would deliver them. That's because hiring managers want to see the first four phases in action, not a list of courses. Each project needs a real problem from a real person, a tool that's online, an eval, and a one-page case study.

**[PAUSE]**

### PART 4. THE INTERVIEW
