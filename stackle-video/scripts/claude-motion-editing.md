### What all of them cover (the script covers these too)
- Claude Motion writes code and uses no video generation model, so it makes no footage and no AI people.
- You can change one word, number or timing without rebuilding the whole animation.
- It is on the Team and Enterprise plans only.
- You export an MP4, or send it to an editor such as Descript.
- Giving Claude a style reference (screenshots) and an SRT transcript gives a better, timed result (#6, #14).
- In Descript, the tips that come up most are editing by deleting text, removing filler words, Studio Sound and captions.
- In CapCut, the tips that come up most are splitting clips, keyframe zooms, auto captions and changing the shape for phones.

- **Old videos with 2026 in the title.** Metics Media's CapCut video (#11) was uploaded in June 2025 and the Descript video (#12) in March 2025. Video #12 shows the older Descript layout, where Underlord was the AI tools panel.
- **Plan limits are skipped.** No Descript or CapCut tutorial says that Descript Free exports at 720p with a watermark. Video #12 implies 4K export. None gives CapCut Pro's price.

On this account, Design is listed as a still canvas for sites, posters and social posts, with no animation. **Check your Design home screen before filming.** If the template is still there, the Pro and Max paragraph in Part 1 can point to it.
- **Rollout guess.** Video #5 says Motion will reach other plans "in the coming days or weeks". Anthropic has not said this, so the script says "not yet" and gives no date.

## Part 1: What Claude Motion is

**[Graphic 2, full screen]** Three boxes joined by arrows: "Your text, charts, shapes and images", then "Claude writes code", then "MP4 video". Underneath, crossed out: "Video generation model".

*[pause]*

Claude Motion is a new kind of artifact. An artifact is a piece of work that Claude builds next to the chat, such as a document, a dashboard or a slide deck. The Motion kind plays like a short video.

Claude Motion does not use a video generation model. A video generation model is the kind of AI that invents footage from a prompt, such as a clip of a busy street at night. Instead, Claude writes code that moves your own text, charts, shapes and images across the screen. Picture a slide where the numbers count up and the bars grow one by one.

*[pause]*

**[On camera]**

Because the animation is code, you can change one small detail and keep everything else. You can change a single word, a number, or how long a scene stays on screen, and Claude does not rebuild the whole video. The same fact sets the limit: Claude Motion cannot film a person or make a realistic scene for you.

*[pause]*

**[Graphic 3, full screen]** A plan table. Free: no. Pro: no. Max: no. Team: yes, $20 per person a month paid yearly, or $25 paid monthly, smallest team 2 people. Enterprise: yes, after an owner turns it on.

## Part 2: Make your first animation

**[Graphic 4, full screen, screen recording]** The Claude message box. "/motion" is typed. Then the Output menu opens and "Motion" is selected.

*[pause]*

To start, open a normal Claude chat on the website or in the desktop app, not in Claude Code. Type slash motion in the message box, or click Output and choose Motion. You can also open the Artifacts tab, pick a template there, and describe what you want.

*[pause]*

**[Graphic 5, full screen]** The example prompt on screen, with four parts highlighted: who will watch, where it will play, how long, and what shape.

*[pause]*

A good prompt reads like a short brief, not a wish. Tell Claude four things: who will watch the animation, where it will play, how long it should be, and what shape the screen is. Sixteen by nine is the wide shape for YouTube, and nine by sixteen is the tall one for a phone. Then attach the report, the numbers or the images you want Claude to use.

Here is an example for something you built. "Make a thirty-second animation for engineers that explains my weather data project. It will play on LinkedIn with the sound off, so every point must be written on the screen. Use the sixteen by nine shape. Show how the project collects weather data every hour, cleans it with Python, stores it in a database, and shows it on a dashboard. Use only the numbers in the attached file."

*[pause]*

**[On camera]**

Before Claude builds anything, it can ask you a few questions, such as which colors or which style you want. Pick an answer, and the first version usually arrives in a few minutes.

*[pause]*

**[Graphic 6, full screen, screen recording]** The Motion editor: the chat on the left, the animation on the right, and a timeline with one block for each scene.

*[pause]*

When the animation is ready, the chat sits on the left and the animation plays on the right. Below it, a timeline shows one block for each scene. You can make changes in three ways. You can ask Claude in the chat, for example, "Make the dashboard scene two seconds longer." You can click one part of the picture and leave a comment on just that part. Or you can type a new word or number straight into the editor.

*[pause]*

**[On camera]**

Before a big change, make a copy of the animation, so your first version stays safe. And before you post anything, read every word and check every number yourself. Claude can make a mistake, and your name is on the video.

*[pause]*

Two extra attachments make the result much better. Screenshots of a style you like, such as your own website, let Claude match your colors and fonts. ## Part 3: Finish the video in Descript

**[Graphic 8, full screen, screen recording]** Descript's editor. The script on the left looks like a document, and the video is on the right. One sentence is selected and deleted, and the video jumps forward.

*[pause]*

Descript is a video editor that works like a document. It turns everything you say into a written script, and when you delete a word from that script, the word disappears from your recording too.

*[pause]*

**[Graphic 9, full screen, screen recording]** In Claude: Customize, Connectors, Descript, Connect. Then in the animation: Share, the Export tab, Send to, Descript, and the link to the new project.

*[pause]*

To send an animation straight to Descript, you first connect the two apps. In Claude, open Customize and choose Connectors, which are the links between Claude and other apps. Find Descript and click Connect. You only do this once.

Once the apps are connected, open your animation and click Share. Go to the Export tab and pick Descript from the Send to menu. Claude then gives you a link to a new project with your animation inside.

*[pause]*

**[On camera]**

The animation arrives in Descript as a flat video, so you cannot change its words or colors there. Make those changes in Claude, and then send it again.

Now come the four edits in Descript.

**[Card beside you]** Edit 1: Delete words to cut.

Cutting by text is the first edit. Record your voice in Descript, then read the script, select any sentence you do not want, and press Delete. The video is cut at that exact spot. Nothing is lost for good, because you can right-click the cut and restore what you removed.

**[Card beside you]** Edit 2: Remove filler words and long pauses.

Filler words are small sounds like "um" and "uh" that people say while they think. Open the AI Tools panel and choose Remove filler words. Descript lists every one, and you can delete them all or check them one at a time. Turn on Avoid harsh cuts, so it skips any "um" that it cannot cut cleanly. Then run Shorten word gaps from the same panel, which cuts every long silence down to a length you choose, such as a fifth of a second.

**[Card beside you]** Edit 3: Studio Sound.

Studio Sound cleans up your voice. It removes background noise and echo, so a recording made on a laptop microphone in a noisy room comes out much cleaner. Select your script layer, open the Properties panel, and switch on Studio Sound under Audio Effects. If your voice starts to seem strange, move the Intensity slider down.

**[Card beside you]** Edit 4: Captions.

Captions are the words on screen that match what you say. They help anyone who watches with the sound off, and they help people who speak English as a second language. Open the Captions panel and click a style. Descript builds them from your script, so they are already timed to your voice.

*[pause]*

Descript has a free plan with limits. You get sixty minutes of media each month, and your video exports at 720p, which is lower quality than full HD, with a watermark. ## Part 4: Finish the video in CapCut

**[Graphic 12, full screen, screen recording]** CapCut on a computer: the player in the middle, the menus at the top, and the timeline at the bottom.

*[pause]*

CapCut is a video editor with a free plan, made by ByteDance, the company that created TikTok. It is built for short videos, and it works on a computer, in a browser and on a phone. There is no direct link from Claude Motion, so export the MP4 from Claude and import the file into CapCut.

*[pause]*

**[On camera]**

If you live in India, CapCut is not available to you. The Indian government blocked it in 2020, together with dozens of other apps, and the block is still in place. Do these edits in Descript instead.

**[Card beside you]** Edit 1: Split and delete.

Splitting is the most basic edit in CapCut. Move the playhead, which is the line that shows where you are in the video, to the spot you want to cut. Press Control and B on Windows, or Command and B on a Mac, and the clip splits in two. Select the part you do not want and press Delete.

**[Card beside you]** Edit 2: Change the shape for phones.

A wide YouTube video looks small on a phone. Click Ratio and pick nine by sixteen, the tall shape for YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok. Add your text and captions after you change the shape, so nothing ends up outside the screen.

**[Card beside you]** Edit 3: Zoom with keyframes.

A keyframe saves a setting, such as the size of the picture, at one moment. When you set two of them, CapCut moves smoothly between the two settings. For example, to zoom in on a line of code, select the clip and click the small diamond next to Scale, the size setting. Move the playhead one second later, make the clip bigger, and click the diamond again. The video now zooms in slowly over that second.

**[Card beside you]** Edit 4: Auto captions.

CapCut can write the words on screen for you. Open Captions, choose Auto captions, pick the language you speak in the video, and click Generate. Picking the right language matters, because the wrong one gives you wrong words or nothing at all. Read every line afterwards, and fix names and technical terms by hand.

*[pause]*

**[On camera]**

CapCut's Pro plan costs nineteen dollars and ninety-nine cents a month, and it adds premium templates, more AI credits and cloud storage. CapCut has changed which features are free more than once, so check what your plan includes before you rely on auto captions.

*[pause]*

## Which editor to pick

**[Graphic 13, full screen]** Two columns. Descript: "Most of the video is you talking. Edit the text." CapCut: "Short, tall clips with zooms, music and bold captions." Bottom line: "Claude Motion makes the animation. The editor finishes the video."

*[pause]*

Pick Descript when most of your video is you talking, such as a tutorial, a project walkthrough or a longer YouTube video. Editing the text is much faster than cutting a timeline, and the direct link from Claude Motion saves you a step.

Pick CapCut when you want a short, tall clip for a phone, with zooms, music and bold captions. You do not need both editors. Choose the one that fits the video you make most often.
