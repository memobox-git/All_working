# RAG Short, told the Storyworthy way

**Framework:** Matthew Dicks, *Storyworthy*. A story is about a five-second moment in which someone changes. Start at the opposite of where you end, put the stakes up front, let the audience carry the plan with the character, and slow down just before the moment.

**Character:** Maya, a support lead. She is a made-up character, told in the third person: Storyworthy works best with a true story of your own, and this script doesn't claim one. If you have a real moment like this, it can replace hers.

**Length:** 151 spoken words, 55 seconds. Vertical 1080×1920.

**Status:** made. The video is `stackle-video/graphics/rag-short-storyworthy.mp4` (captions in `.srt`), built from `stackle-video/source/rag-short-storyworthy/` on the shared kit in `rag-short/`. Maya is a 3D character made in Canva ([open](https://www.canva.com/M/MAHYqWtUyZg)).

| Storyworthy tool | Where |
|---|---|
| Stakes up front ("elephant in the room") | Lines 1 and 2: a customer was promised 60 days; the policy is 30 |
| Start at the opposite of the end | Line 3: Maya is sure the fix is a bigger model. Line 14: she now asks "has it read your documents?" |
| The plan the audience carries ("backpack") | Line 3: "So she pays for one." We hope it works with her |
| Slow down before the moment ("hourglass") | Lines 4 to 6: three short lines, one beat each |
| The five-second moment | Lines 7 and 8: "The model isn't dumb. It has never read her refund policy." |
| Show the change, don't state it | Line 14: what she says now, to someone else |

## Script

| # | Voice | Scene |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Maya's AI support bot just told a customer they can get a refund after sixty days. | Maya, then her bot's chat: "Refunds within 60 days" |
| 2 | Her company's policy is thirty. | The policy page, "30 days" circled, against the bot's 60 |
| 3 | Maya is sure the fix is a bigger model. So she pays for one. | The model card grows to 700B, "upgraded" |
| 4 | Same question. | The chat again, the question typed |
| 5 | Same confident answer. | The bot typing |
| 6 | Sixty days. | "60 days" again. A red "still made up" stamp |
| 7 | And that's when it hits her. | Maya, with a "!" |
| 8 | The model isn't dumb. It has never read her refund policy. | The locked files: "never read" |
| 9 | So she tries a trick called RAG. | The robot librarian and R·A·G |
| 10 | Before the bot answers, it searches her documents, grabs the three paragraphs that match best, and reads them first. | The lens sweeps the files, then the three best paragraphs |
| 11 | Every paragraph is stored as numbers that capture its meaning, so it finds the right one even when customers use different words. | Numbers into the database, then the map of meaning |
| 12 | Now every answer comes with its source. | "Refunds within 30 days [1]" and the source |
| 13 | And when the policy changes, she just updates the document. No retraining. | 30 becomes 14, "Retrain the model" crossed out |
| 14 | Now, when someone tells Maya they need a bigger model, she asks one question: has it read your documents? | Maya, with the question in a speech bubble |
| 15 | Follow. Next, I'll explain AI agents. | The waving robot, "Next: AI agents", Follow |
