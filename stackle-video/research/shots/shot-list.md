# The shot list: what the competition films, and how Stackle copies it

We studied 37 top Shorts from 17 channels on 16 October 2026: 19 from data and AI career channels, and 18 from tech career channels and Hormozi. YouTube blocked video downloads, so every shot was read from YouTube's own preview frames. Views are exact counts from that day. The full notes are in `data-career-channels.md` and `tech-career-channels.md`. The picture strips are in `frames/`.

See `shot-board.jpg` for one frame of each shot below.

---

## What every top Short does, whatever the shot

1. **Text on screen from the first second.** 14 of the 17 tech Shorts had readable hook text in their first second. All three of Sundas's biggest Shorts had it on the very first frame.
2. **A fixed title at the top**, which stays for the whole Short and works like a title inside the video.
3. **Captions at chest height**, 1–4 words at a time, bold, white with one key word in colour. They never sit at the very bottom, where the app buttons cover them.
4. **The mic shows.** 13 of the 19 data career Shorts show a mic: a podcast mic, a handheld mic or a headset. It reads as "expert".
5. **Calm editing.** In the data career group, the typical Short changes picture about 1.5 times every 10 seconds. Five Shorts had almost no cuts and still reached 205K–2.2M views. The hook and the format matter more than fast cutting.
6. **The Short ends on the payoff**, like the answer, the reveal or a reaction. Almost none end with "subscribe".
7. **Most run 30–60 seconds.** The tech group's middle length was 54 seconds.

## The 12 shots

| # | Shot | Best example | How to film it | How to edit it | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Close-up talking head** | Hormozi "Always Be Ready", 8.8M | Seated, phone on a tripod at chest height, 1 metre away. Frame from the top of the head to the chest. Window light, a real room behind you. | Cut the pauses. Zoom in on every second or third sentence. Big captions on the chest. Pop in a picture when you name a thing. | Low |
| 2 | **Numbered list with a fixed top title** | Sundas "Don't Become a Data Analyst if", 3.5M | Same as shot 1, with a podcast mic in the frame. Film 3–4 extra clips of 3 seconds each: hands typing, your screen, you at the desk from the side. | Two-line title at the top for the whole Short. A small label for each point ("#1 On-call"). One short extra clip inside each point. | Low |
| 3 | **Board that fills in** | Sajjaad's Amazon salary board, 2.8M | Same as shot 1 but a bit wider, with space left for the board. The phone stays locked so the board lines up. | Show the empty board at second 0 (years, levels, X vs Y). Drop in one number each time you say it. Save the biggest number for the end. | Low (build the board template once) |
| 4 | **Diagram that comes back** | Zach Wilson "Master the 5 levels of SQL", 427K | Shot 1 framing. | One simple diagram (a pyramid, a level list, steps 1-2-3) full screen between sections, filling in one step at a time. | Low |
| 5 | **Mock interview split** | Sumit Mittal "Parquet", 243K; The Big Data Show "Explaining your project", 307K | Record a real mock interview on Zoom or Google Meet. Webcam quality is fine. One common question per Short. | Interviewer on top, the person answering below. Captions on the line between them. Optional 2-second card with the question first. Almost no cuts. | Very low |
| 6 | **Event interview with a handheld mic** | Sundas "How much money Software Engineers make?", 4.9M; "Data Engineer vs Data Scientist", 2.2M | At a meetup or conference. A friend holds the phone at chest height, or it sits on a tripod for a wide shot. Hold a wireless mic towards each person. | Question in a yellow box at the top. Show each answer (salary, skills) as text over the person who said it. End on the reveal. | Low edit, needs people |
| 7 | **Face-off with a guest** | Sajjaad "Software Engineer vs Data Science", 3.0M | Two people side by side, one mic passed between them, phone 1.5–2 metres away. 3–5 quick questions. | A role label on each person ("Data Engineer" vs "AI Engineer"). Answers stack under each one. | Low edit, needs a guest |
| 8 | **Challenge for a prize** | Sajjaad "Comp Sci Challenge for $700", 7.1M | One continuous take. You hold the phone and the prize. The other person explains a topic in 30 seconds. | Fixed title ("Explain Kafka in 30 seconds for $50"). Almost no cuts. End on the handover. | Very low edit, needs people and a prize |
| 9 | **POV skit** | Andrew Codesmith "can't work from home anymore", 3.1M | Your face reacting, then "the other side": a laptop on a fake call, a Slack message, an error screen. Film your lines, then film the other side. | "POV: you're a data engineer and…" at the top. A cut on every line. End on your reaction. | Medium (needs a script) |
| 10 | **Prop that shows the idea** | Hormozi "Build the Right Foundation" (toy bricks), 9.5M | Pick one object that stands for the idea and do the action for real. Film it from 3–4 angles. | Cut between the angles. Big centred captions. | Medium filming, low edit |
| 11 | **Screen walkthrough, you still visible** | Ashish's resume walkthrough, 1.5M; Satish filming his screen, 2.4M | Film your monitor over your shoulder while you point, or record the screen and add your face in a corner. | Zoom into the part you're talking about. Highlight it in yellow. Bring your face back every 10 seconds. | Low |
| 12 | **Skit where you play both people** | Jash Radia "Data Engineering Interviews in 2021 vs 2025", 189K | Film yourself twice, in two shirts and two places, both in webcam framing. | Stack both clips as a fake video call. Title cards for the two parts. End on the punchline. | Medium |

## Stackle's starter set

These fit Chris best. He can film most of them alone. He also has a meetup community of about 3,900 people and runs career calls, which is where shots 5, 6 and 8 come from.

| Order | Shot | Why it fits Stackle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **2. List + fixed top title** | It's the format of the biggest data career Short we found (3.5M). It needs no other people, and it suits every "rejection reason" and "don't become" Short. |
| 2 | **5. Mock interview split** | Chris runs interviews for a living. Sumit Mittal and The Big Data Show get 162K–307K per question with no editing. |
| 3 | **6. Event interview** | It's the top format by views in the data group (4.9M and 2.2M). Film it at the next Stackle meetup. |
| 4 | **3. Board that fills in** | Money and levels: W2 vs 1099, services vs product pay, the 4 levels of projects. |
| 5 | **1. Close-up talking head** | Chris's own stories, like the Amazon interview, in Hormozi's style. |
| 6 | **10. Prop** | AI terms made physical: tokens as toy bricks, the context window as a box that fills up. |
| Later | 8, 9, 12 | The challenge at a meetup, and the POV skits once the basics run smoothly. |

## Gear for all 12 shots

- A phone and a tripod.
- A wireless mic kit with two transmitters. One can be held as a handheld mic for shots 6–8.
- One soft light, or a window beside you.
- A podcast mic on a desk stand. It is a prop as much as a mic.
- Props for shot 10: toy bricks and a clear box.
- A second shirt for shot 12.

## Editing tools

The recipes above use auto-captions, zooms and text boxes. Any editor does this: Descript, or CapCut in the US. CapCut is blocked in India, so editors based there should use Descript or VN.
