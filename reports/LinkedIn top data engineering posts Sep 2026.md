# Top data engineering LinkedIn posts, September 2026 (200+ reactions)

We found **126 LinkedIn posts from September 2026 with at least 200 reactions**. Of those, **13 are about data engineering interviews** and **104 are about data engineering tools**, platforms and core concepts. Another 9 are popular data engineering posts that fit neither group. Every count was read from the post's own public page on 19 October 2026, and every date comes from the post's ID, so both are exact. The biggest tool post was Liam Wynne's launch of ERD Studio, a VS Code extension for data modeling (**2,550 reactions, 202 comments**). Next came Michael Drogalis on Kafka 4.2's new queues (**1,799**). The biggest interview post was a "design a pipeline for 1 TB a day" challenge from Tech Interview Titans (**961**).

One gap matters. Coverage is good for **17–30 September**, but posts from **1–16 September** are mostly missing. Of about 8,000 September posts we reached, only 128 were from 1–16 September, and only 7 of those made the list.

## Interview posts

SQL dominated. Nine of the 13 interview posts are SQL question lists or a single "asked at company X" scenario. The other four cover designing a pipeline for 1 TB a day, partitioning, ETL vs ELT, and Delta tables. No interview-experience story ("I cleared / I got rejected at X") passed 200 in this window.

## What the top posts have in common

- **A launch or a clear explainer beats a hot take.** The biggest tool posts were a free tool built by a practitioner (ERD Studio), an insider's explanation of a release (Kafka queues, dbt v2), and a plain explainer of the medallion architecture.
- **Lists and cheat sheets travel.** "50 PySpark commands", "SQL vs Pandas: 50 operations", "100 SQL questions", "20 Databricks resources" and "Top 50 Spark performance issues" all passed 400.
- **"X vs Y" works for tools.** Examples are Fabric vs Databricks, Kafka vs SQS vs RabbitMQ, lake vs warehouse vs lakehouse, and Pandas vs DuckDB vs Polars vs Spark. Copies of the same post cleared 200 several times: Fabric vs Databricks (Yash Jadiya, Monita B), "Data engineer DNA" (Gowtham SB, Yuktha Reddy Vanteru), and view vs CTE vs temporary table (Lovee Kumar, Romesh Kesharwani).
- **Interview posts win with one concrete question.** The format is "asked at Accenture", "count the rows without running the query" or "design for 1 TB a day", not general advice.
- **News spreads through several voices.** Databricks' Row Zero deal produced four separate posts over 200, and FabCon produced at least five.

## How we found and checked these posts

Web search could not find recent LinkedIn posts. Its index held almost nothing newer than June 2026, and LinkedIn blocks profile pages for logged-out visitors. Each public post page does carry its exact reaction and comment counts, plus a list of about ten related recent posts. We started from a few September posts, plus vendor and company pages such as Databricks, dbt Labs and DataVidhya, and followed those related lists. That reached about 8,000 distinct September posts, and we read the reaction count shown for almost all of them. LinkedIn's related list only covers roughly the last 30 days, which is why early September is thin. The date of each post comes from its LinkedIn activity ID, whose top bits are the publish time in milliseconds.

**What we left out.** Some posts passed 200 reactions but were about data analysis, Excel, Power BI or general Python, and we removed them. We also left out several general SQL posts not aimed at data engineers. Those include "Advanced MySQL cheat sheet" by Cornixe (659), "EXISTS vs IN" by Rhythm (589), "WHERE 1=1" by Harshni Desai (524), "Pandas vs SQL" by Carol Mello (490) and "ROW_NUMBER vs RANK" by Abhishek Gupta (206). We excluded B-trees in databases by Kritika Goyal (716) and AI-agent posts that are not about data tooling.

**What we counted.** Reshares that only repeat the original's numbers were dropped; for example, DataVidhya resharing Darshil Parmar's posts. So were posts where the page showed no count. LinkedIn's "reactions" count every reaction type, not only likes. The full list, with activity IDs, is in `research_notes/LinkedIn top data engineering posts Sep 2026/posts.csv`.
