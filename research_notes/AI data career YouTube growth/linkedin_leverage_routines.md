# Using a large LinkedIn audience to grow on YouTube, and the operating routines of fast-growing educational creators (for Stackle / Chris)

Research date: 15 October 2026. Labels: **CLAIMED** = the creator's own statement, not independently verified. **ESTIMATED** = my inference or arithmetic. **MEASURED** = numbers I pulled myself from public YouTube channel pages with yt-dlp on 15 Oct 2026. YouTube shows subscriber counts rounded to 3 significant figures. Flat-list view totals are approximate and leave out live streams.

Method note: I pulled channel data and auto-transcripts with yt-dlp (`player_client=android`). After about 15 transcript pulls, YouTube began returning "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot", so a few planned transcripts (Colin and Samir, Paddy Galloway, the $100M Leads audiobook) could not be fetched. Those are listed as gaps.

## Q1. Which tech/data/AI career creators had big LinkedIn audiences before or alongside YouTube, and how fast did their YouTube grow?

### Takeaway
Several data/AI creators have both a large LinkedIn audience and a 120K–360K YouTube channel: Zach Wilson, Aishwarya Srinivasan, Sundas Khalid, Seattle Data Guy, Darshil Parmar, Ankit Bansal and Shashank Mishra. None of them shows evidence of LinkedIn followers converting directly into subscribers. YouTube growth came from YouTube-native hits, usually free full courses, end-to-end projects or clear explainers. - ESTIMATED: The audience mix seems to shape the format. India-heavy channels (Darshil, Ankit, Shashank) grew mainly through long-form tutorials with very few Shorts. US-skewed personal-brand channels (Sundas, Zach, Aishwarya) get 39–76% of their views from Shorts. Chris's audience spans both, which argues for one weekly long-form tutorial or career video plus Shorts.
- ESTIMATED: Zach's playbook is the closest model for Chris. LinkedIn authority came first; a free, time-boxed, daily YouTube course was then launched and promoted to the existing audience, and homework credit was routed into his paid platform.

## Q2. What specific tactics moved people from LinkedIn (and other platforms) to YouTube?

### Takeaway
The documented tactics are a launch event announced to the existing audience (Zach's free bootcamp), testing ideas in short text and expanding the winners into long-form (Hormozi), keeping the LinkedIn and newsletter cadence running alongside YouTube (Jeff Su), and talking directly with your most engaged followers (Zach). I found no first-hand data comparing clip-plus-link-in-comments, LinkedIn Live or LinkedIn newsletters as YouTube drivers.

### Cited Findings
- **Launch event to the existing audience.** Zach Wilson announced the free YouTube bootcamp with a fixed start date, a daily cadence (15 Nov to 31 Dec) and an explicit share request ([X, Nov 2024](https://x.com/Zachly/status/1855409271254725056)). The launch video has 409K views (MEASURED). He repeated the format with a 6-week edition ([Jun 2025](https://blog.dataexpert.io/p/how-to-join-the-free-6-week-data)) and an AI/Databricks edition in Aug 2026 ([Substack note](https://substack.com/@eczachly/note/c-301596993)).
- **Talking to your most engaged followers.** Zach "crawled his own LinkedIn posts to find people who engaged often, then spoke with them about what they liked and disliked". He says the metric is "how many conversations you are having with the people", not engagement counts ([Dutch Engineer, May 2023](https://dutchengineer.substack.com/p/ask-a-data-mentor-zach-wilson-founder)). His DataExpert LinkedIn course's first lesson was titled "Build community, not content" (search-result snippet; the [course page](https://www.dataexpert.io/program/linkedin-mastery-course) now returns "Program not found").
- **Testing ideas in text, then expanding the winners.** A secondary account says Hormozi tests concepts on Twitter, and "the Tweets that earn the most engagement are repurposed into long-form content" ([Copyblogger](https://copyblogger.com/content-repurposing/), undated). Not verified against a Hormozi primary source.
- **Keeping LinkedIn and the newsletter alongside YouTube.** Jeff Su: "I'll keep doing [YouTube videos]... I'll keep writing my newsletter. I keep writing LinkedIn posts." He keeps "Googler turned educator" in his LinkedIn headline to borrow the Google brand ([Ali Abdaal, 21 May 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LLjpnubsOWc)). In the same interview he describes advising a friend to post on LinkedIn, add the book link, start a newsletter and chop the book into lead magnets. The first sale came about 2 months after starting the newsletter. CLAIMED.
- **Daily LinkedIn volume.** Ali Abdaal tells his academy students: "you're going to have to post every day on LinkedIn" plus "200 connection requests a week" ([21 May 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LLjpnubsOWc)). This is advice for selling, not specifically for driving YouTube.
- **Cross-platform discovery works between formats, not within a funnel.** Hormozi: "shorts viewers watch more shorts. And long viewers watch more longs. And customers buy more customers." He adds that "the magic happens" when someone finds you on TikTok but is "a longs viewer on YouTube", and "making shorts in order for people to on a different platform watch longs still carries as far as we know" ([compilation, 14 Mar 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jmkq5RLjm0U)). CLAIMED, from his own team's data.
- **Ask for one action and keep the pitch on-topic.** Hormozi defines influence as "high likelihood of compliance with requests". He builds it by stacking status, power (following his instructions produced results), credibility (third-party proof) and likeness. His acronym for this is "SPCL" ([Hormozi, 18 Oct 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dMZ-n2KSlxE)).
- **LinkedIn's own video push (context).**
  - LinkedIn said video uploads rose 36% YoY and is testing a vertical video feed ([TechCrunch, 4 Feb 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/04/linkedin-amps-up-vertical-video-tools-as-uploads-jump-36)).
  - Digiday reported 34% YoY growth in uploads (FQ4 2024) and 36% growth in viewership (FQ1 2025), and weekly video creation by publisher-program members up 67% ([Digiday, 2025](https://digiday.com/marketing/linkedins-video-push-appears-to-be-working-in-2025/)).
  - The two outlets give slightly different upload figures.

### Inferences
- ESTIMATED: The best-evidenced lever for Chris is an event, not a link in individual posts. For example, a free "14-day Data Engineer / AI Engineer bootcamp" on YouTube, announced repeatedly to 200K LinkedIn followers with a fixed start date and daily episodes, and tied to the free career call or Masterclass (the Zach pattern).
- ESTIMATED: Chris's LinkedIn post analytics can act as a free idea-testing lab. Text posts with outlier engagement become YouTube long-form topics (the Hormozi pattern). Long-form transcripts in turn feed LinkedIn posts and Shorts.
- ESTIMATED: Native LinkedIn video clips of the long-form (vertical, captioned, text matching the video) fit the format LinkedIn says it is growing. Whether they convert to YouTube subscribers is untested in any source I found.

### Gaps
- No primary data on conversion from LinkedIn native clips, "link in first comment", LinkedIn Live (or simulcasting to YouTube), or LinkedIn newsletters to YouTube subscribers.
- No first-person "how I moved my LinkedIn audience to YouTube" account from Aishwarya Srinivasan, Sundas Khalid, Darshil Parmar, Ankit Bansal or Shashank Mishra. Searches returned only profiles.

## Q3. What does LinkedIn's algorithm do with external links in 2025–2026, and how do creators work around it?

### Takeaway
LinkedIn has never officially said it demotes links. Its March 2026 feed update targets engagement bait ("comment to agree"), pods, comment automation and "videos that don't match the text". Large third-party studies find an average reach drop of about 19–42% for posts with links, mostly on company pages. Personal profiles show little or no penalty, and the old "link in first comment" workaround has stopped being free.

### Cited Findings
- **Official LinkedIn statement, 12 Mar 2026.** LinkedIn will "reduce repetitive, low‑substance posts and engagement bait", including "comment to agree" prompts; cut "recycled and click‑driven posts"; "make engagement pods ineffective and curb comment automation"; and target "videos that don't match the text". It wants more posts that "offer genuine insight, actionable ideas, and thoughtful perspectives". The post does not mention external links ([LinkedIn News, 12 Mar 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed)).
- **Other 2026 LinkedIn announcements.** A follow-up post on 13 Mar 2026 addressed pods and automated comments, and a 15 Sep 2026 post covered classifiers limiting "AI slop". Both are as summarised by [Sociallyin](https://sociallyin.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-authenticity-update/); I did not read the originals.
- **Ordinal study.**
  - Link posts averaged a 26.5% reach penalty, growing from about 5% (2023) to about 42% (2025). Link posts earn higher engagement per impression ([Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-link-penalty-study)). Search summaries give the sample as ~900,000 posts, Feb 2023–Feb 2026; the page I fetched did not state the size.
  - Company pages take most of the penalty; personal profiles show "almost none" in segmented data.
  - First-comment links "stopped being free somewhere around 2024" and get far fewer clicks than links in the body. "If clicks are the goal", the page recommends the body link (same source).
- **Other studies.**
  - LinkPost (2026, cited by Ordinal): median 448 impressions for link posts vs 705 without, a 36% gap.
  - Richard van der Blom: one link cost 18.8% of median reach (Apr 2025); 4+ links cost about 24% ([Ordinal summary](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-link-penalty-study); [AgilityPR](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-news/social-media-influencer-marketing/the-linkedin-link-tax-why-sharing-your-blog-is-costing-you-60-percent-of-your-audience/)).
  - Claims of a "60%" penalty are unsourced.
- **Counter-view.** ContentIn notes that LinkedIn "has never published a policy stating that a post is demoted simply for containing a link" ([ContentIn glossary](https://contentin.io/glossary/external-link-penalty/)). A Q1 2026 "Saywhat" analysis with unpublished methods reportedly found multi-link posts doing better ([Vivian Voss blog](https://www.vivianvoss.net/blog/linkedin-reach-you-did-not-earn)). Treat both as weak.
- **"Comment X to get the link" posts.** Critics describe this as bait ([Christian Heilmann](https://christianheilmann.com/?p=9794)). LinkedIn's official wording names "comment to agree", not "comment for link", so I have not seen it explicitly confirmed that comment-for-link posts are demoted.

### Inferences
- ESTIMATED: For Chris's personal profile, the link penalty is probably modest. The best-supported options are to put the YouTube link in the body when clicks matter (launch days), or to post link-free value posts that point people to search "Chris + Stackle on YouTube". The featured section and profile website field can carry the evergreen link.
- ESTIMATED: Avoid leaning on "comment 'SQL' and I'll DM you" as the main mechanic. It is close to the "engagement bait" LinkedIn said it is cutting in March 2026, and automating those DMs risks the "comment automation" crackdown.
- ESTIMATED: Native video on LinkedIn must match its post text (LinkedIn names "videos that don't match the text"). A clip with a substantive text summary fits the stated rules better than a generic teaser.

### Gaps
- No primary LinkedIn statement on links either way. All penalty figures come from vendors or practitioners with partly undisclosed samples.
- No study isolates whether a YouTube link specifically (vs any domain) is treated differently.

## Q4. Alex Hormozi's documented content model (Chris's style model)

### Takeaway
Hormozi's model has four parts:
- **Volume:** CLAIMED 35,000 pieces and 1,000 hours on camera over about 40 months, costing $4M.
- **Education over edutainment:** clear packaging, intros that prove you can deliver, and success judged by revenue per view rather than raw views.
- **Long-form as the main converter:** Shorts are kept at volume for reach.
- **Staged outsourcing:** a cheap agency first, then a premium agency, then an in-house team.

He has moved toward more research up front and less editing, and in late 2025 toward live streams.

### Cited Findings
- **Scale and inputs (CLAIMED).**
  - "$4 million in team, equipment, vendors, studios, and software, a thousand hours of me on camera recording, and 35,000 pieces of content" over "40-ish months". His original plan was: "make as much good stuff as I could… post it everywhere I could… learn as much as I could" ([compilation, 14 Mar 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jmkq5RLjm0U)).
  - In Oct 2025 he said "I posted 35,000 pieces of content this year" and "32.7 million views on YouTube this month" ([18 Oct 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dMZ-n2KSlxE)). The two "35,000" statements conflict: "this year" vs "40 months".
- **Six lessons from those 40 months (CLAIMED, [14 Mar 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jmkq5RLjm0U)):**
  1. "edutainment to education"
  2. "for us to for you"
  3. "wide to narrow"
  4. "views to revenue"
  5. "shorts to longs"
  6. "assume more to assume nothing"

  Details from the same talk:
  - **Packaging, vague to clear:** thumbnails and titles say exactly what the video is.
  - **Intros:** in education, the intro should show "that you can do what you promised them in the thumbnail". It should be proof, not a repeat of the title. He rewatched his "top 35 educational videos" to work this out.
  - **"Post-production to pre-search":** "if we spent a quarter of the time that we used to spend on post in pre, we eliminate 90%, maybe 95% of our post edit work".
  - **Views to revenue:** he tracks RPM / ad revenue per video as the quality-plus-quantity metric. The month with the highest RPM "also created the most book sales, the most opt-ins, and the most business applications". In a 90-day test of mostly edutainment, "views went through the roof… 2, 3X" but "ad revenue dropped by half. So we're actually getting the wrong people."
  - **Shorts to longs:** "longs drive a lot more conversions than shorts. Conversions being book sales, opt-ins, and applications". The plan is to "keep the volume the same" on Shorts but make them more business-focused.
  - **Re-post old winners:** new audience members have not seen them. Title for strangers: "title it like they don't know you, introduce yourself, say why they should listen".
- **Outsourcing path (CLAIMED, same video).**
  - Phase 1: a "basic agency" at about $3–5K/month for one brand on one platform. "They just got me to commit to doing three videos a week. They just trimmed it… put an intro, and a thumbnail", for 3–6 months.
  - Then premium agencies at $15–30K/month. Then he brought the SOPs in-house: his short-form "director of brand" came from a premium agency.
  - TikTok started with an agency repurposing existing long-form into Shorts at no work to him.
  - "Usually, you master one platform… then you can more quickly pay to learn the other ones… that process is how we approached LinkedIn."
- **Shorts vs long-form maths (CLAIMED, [18 Oct 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dMZ-n2KSlxE)).**
  - "I see the purpose of shorts as many times a way to get someone to watch a long."
  - "Someone would have to watch 480 shorts to have the same level of exposure as watching 2 hours with me."
  - "We just know that like one out of 10… shorts is going to be a two or three [X] outlier… Same thing for longs."
  - "The content is the targeting. The algorithm is so good now."
  - "Live interactive" is his stated focus going forward.
  - On realistic expectations: "I have two businesses that I looked at in the last year that were doing over a million dollars a year with less than 5,000 followers."
- **2026–27 production philosophy (CLAIMED, [15 Aug 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZTSI3DDP_4A)).**
  - He applies "The Algorithm" (question requirements → delete → simplify → accelerate → automate) to content.
  - They deleted colour grading. An iPhone video shot on a plane "became a… one out of 10 video because the thing that mattered is the stuff in the video". The aim is "quality volume" ("qualium").
  - "Education is the door"; "we're in the show-me times, not the tell-me times"; "state the facts and tell the truth… The truth sells the most."
  - "The only thing that is more efficient than automation is elimination."
- **Rule of 100.** Do 100 primary actions a day; in content terms, 100 minutes of content creation a day. His own podcast ([The Game Ep 248](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/the-rule-of-100-ep-248)) frames it as "if you do 100 of something every day, you'll likely get better". The book wording is not verified (secondary summaries: [dcubed](https://dcubed.substack.com/p/the-rule-of-100-why-consistency-matters)).
- **Third-party clip-count claims (unverified, conflicting).** Vendor blogs claim "250+ short-form pieces" per long-form, "30 to 50" clips per 60-minute recording, a "team of 20+ editors" and "1 to 3 long-form pieces per day" ([ReachCat, 20 Apr 2026](https://reach.cat/blog/hormozi-clipping-strategy-book-launch/)). None cite a source, and the article contradicts itself on ad spend.
- **Content Accordion.** His Mar 2025 episode "My Approach To Scalable Content – The Content Accordion" covers quality vs quantity and broad vs narrow topics. He started with Facebook Lives for gym owners, "rip[ped] off the audio" into a podcast from July 2017, and in 2020 used a YouTube vendor that only trimmed podcast episodes ([Audioscrape listing](https://audioscrape.com/podcast/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/episode/my-approach-to-scalable-content---the-content-accordion--ep-845)). The full transcript is behind a sign-in.

### Inferences
- ESTIMATED: For Chris, the Hormozi model translates as follows:
  1. Make one long-form "pillar" a week in Hormozi style. Lead with proof (200+ engineers interviewed, real placement stories), not hype.
  2. Use the long-form to source Shorts.
  3. Judge success by career-call bookings per video (his RPM equivalent), not views.
  4. Keep production light: no colour grading, raw is fine.
  5. Put the time saved into pre-search (outline, proof, visuals).
- ESTIMATED: Hormozi's "shorts viewers ≠ long viewers" finding means Shorts should not be judged on how many people they send to long-form. Judge them on reach and on whether they bring the right avatar (data analysts, testers, junior engineers) into the funnel.

## Q5. What do fast-growing educational creators actually do day to day? (scripting, batching, idea sourcing, team, hours, analytics)

### Takeaway
The documented routines share a pattern:
- Lock the title and thumbnail before scripting.
- Spend most of the time in pre-production (Jeff Su: 8–10 of ~20 hours per video).
- Film fast (about 1 hour per video).
- Outsource editing early.
- Capture ideas immediately and work from checklists.

Part-time creators reported 4–8 hours a week (Sundas Khalid, Ali Abdaal's template) up to roughly 45–55 hours a week (Jeff Su while at Google, ESTIMATED from his stated schedule).

## Q6. What weekly long-form vs Shorts cadence did fast growers use, and what is realistic for a solo founder with a business to run?

### Takeaway
Documented cadences cluster around 1–2 long-form videos a week plus 2–5+ Shorts:
- Paddy Galloway (2022): 2 long + 2 Shorts.
- Paddy's reported newer playbook: 1 long + 3+ Shorts.
- Jeff Su while at Google: 1–2 long.
- Ali Abdaal's part-time template: 1 a week at about 8 hours.
- Hormozi's starter agency phase: 3 a week.

For a solo founder, 1 long-form plus about 5–7 Shorts a week, cross-posted, at roughly 8–12 hours of founder time with an editor, is consistent with these sources (ESTIMATED).

### Cited Findings
- Paddy Galloway: 2 long + 2 Shorts a week ([2022](https://theviralpost.beehiiv.com/p/guide-to-100000-youtube-subscribers)). Reported newer playbook: 1 long (15–40 min) + 3+ Shorts a week ([X article, unverified snippet](https://x.com/PaddyG96/article/2074484301777785184)).
- Jeff Su: "one or two videos a week" while employed full time, at about 20 hours per video ([21 May 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LLjpnubsOWc)).
- Ali Abdaal: 1 video a week, about 8 hours a week, outsourced editing ([Recall summary](https://www.recall.it/summary/ali-abdaal/if-i-started-youtube-while-working-full-time-id-do-this)).
- Hormozi: a basic agency had him commit to "three videos a week" for the first 3–6 months ([14 Mar 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jmkq5RLjm0U)).
- Aishwarya Srinivasan: weekly upload frequency during her 2026 acceleration ([developereducators, Oct 2026](https://developereducators.com/channel/aishwaryasrinivasan/)).
- Zach Wilson: a daily cadence, time-boxed to a 6-week launch event (15 Nov–31 Dec 2024) ([X](https://x.com/Zachly/status/1855409271254725056)).
- Sundas Khalid: about 4–5 hours a week of her own time, supported by a VA and editors ([LTN, 2025](https://ec.ltn.com.tw/article/breakingnews/5201342)).
- Jay Clouse: weekly video was hard to sustain with heavy post-production; considered about 3 a month ([Nov 2025](https://pod.wave.co/podcast/creator-science-dc8ffac2-9d4b-4034-a53c-bc78b533d35a/281-prioritizing-video-in-podcasting-voice-memo)).
- Hormozi on volume: "it's actually far more linear than you would expect… one out of ten… shorts is going to be a two or three X outlier" ([18 Oct 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dMZ-n2KSlxE)).

### Inferences
ESTIMATED: here is a realistic week for Chris, built from the above.

- **Monday, about 2 hours:** pick next week's long-form topic from LinkedIn outliers and career-call questions. Lock the title and thumbnail concept (Jeff Su's gate).
- **Tuesday, about 2–3 hours:** finish the Hormozi-style script, opening with proof (Hormozi's "show-me" intro). Write 6–10 Short scripts: 3–5 lifted from the long-form, plus standalone hooks.
- **Wednesday, one filming block of about 2–3 hours:** 1 long-form (about 1 hour, per Jeff Su) plus 6–10 Shorts in one setup. No colour grading (Hormozi, Aug 2026).
- **Thursday–Friday, about 1–2 hours:** brief and review the editor's work. Publish the long-form. Schedule Shorts about daily across YouTube Shorts, Reels and TikTok.
- **Daily, about 30–45 minutes:** one LinkedIn post (text or native video that matches the text). Reply to comments. No pods or comment automation (LinkedIn, Mar 2026).
- **Weekly review, about 30 minutes:** track career-call bookings per video and subscribers per 1K views, not raw views (Hormozi's revenue-per-view lesson). Track output metrics (Ali).
- **Quarterly:** run one "free bootcamp" event announced to the LinkedIn audience (Zach's pattern).

Total: about 9–12 founder hours a week, with editing outsourced. A Hormozi-style basic agency costs about $3–5K/month; Indian freelance editors are likely much cheaper (not sourced).
