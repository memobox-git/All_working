Sources:
- the verified posts in [`hooks-swipe-file.md`](hooks-swipe-file.md)
- format benchmarks in [`research/algorithm-and-formats-2026.md`](research/algorithm-and-formats-2026.md)

- the images from top data and AI posts, opened and studied one by one

## 1. What the numbers say

**It depends on the niche and the kind of post.** Media type below was checked inside the main post only. A first pass also counted media from the "related posts" cards under each post, which made text-only posts look like video or image posts; those counts were thrown out.

**Data creators** (196 verified posts with 100+ reactions, at most 12 per creator):

| Media | Posts | Median reactions | Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| text-only | 58 | 536 | 14,528 |
| image (incl. GIF) | 105 | 533 | 6,231 |
| document / carousel | 10 | 516 | 1,748 |
| link preview card | 6 | 460 | 1,159 |
| native video | 16 | 330 | 1,613 |

- **In the data niche, text-only ties with images.** Text-only also holds the biggest post: Zach Wilson's comp post at 14,528. Images dominate memes, salary graphics, SQL puzzle screenshots and infographics.
- **AI creators:** 91 of 104 winners checked were single-image posts: infographics, diagrams, memes. Andrew Ng's text-only story (21,964) is the big exception.
- **Growth sellers:** most of their verified top posts carry an image (Justin Welsh: 100% quote-card images).
- **All three samples are selected for 100+ reactions.** They show which formats *reach* high numbers, not the average result by format.

**So, by post type:**
- **Story posts** (interview moment, money, founder, hiring side): text-only or one real photo.
- **Teaching posts** (what big tech expects, before/after, X vs Y, an interview answer): one strong image.
- **Carousels:** only for step-by-step walkthroughs.
- **Video:** only for Live clips.
- **Polls:** skip them. They reach far but grow followers poorly and can't be boosted.

The big cross-niche datasets (all topics mixed, by impressions) favour images and documents over text and video:
- **AuthoredUp** (personal profiles, reach multiplier against the profile's own average): polls 1.64x, documents 1.45x, images 1.18x, video 0.86-1.10x, text 0.88x.
- **Taplio** (June 2026, median impressions): image 600, document 462, video 460, text 307.
- **Metricool 2026:** carousels averaged 1,451 impressions against 606 for video; image posts got the most comments per post (2.80).
- **Video per-post reach is down 36% year on year** (AuthoredUp; Socialinsider), because supply now outruns demand.
- **Infographics are 27.4% of the top-1% outlier posts** (Saywhat).
- Richard van der Blom's 2026 scorecard: text + image is "Saveable 5/5. Best for: Infographics. Avoid selfies."
- Growth sellers: Justin Welsh's audited posts are 100% image. Vedika: 70% image, 20% carousel, 10% graphics. Lara Acosta puts a visual on nearly every post.

## 2. The 8 visual types that win in data & AI (with real examples)

### 1) The "X vs Y" comparison infographic
- **Example:** Rakesh Gohel, *AI Agents vs. Agentic AI*, **4,083 reactions** ([post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rakeshgohel01_most-still-think-that-ai-agents-and-agentic-activity-7330934143326781440-vn67)). Alex Xu, *RAG vs Agentic RAG*, **9,290** ([post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/alexxubyte_systemdesign-coding-interviewtips-activity-7344040591753678849-j-6J)).
- **Anatomy:**
  - 4:5 portrait.
  - Header strip with his face, the LinkedIn logo and his @handle.
  - A big title with the key term in a highlighted box.
  - A grid of 4-6 panels: a two-column definition table, a small trend chart, a simple diagram, and a summary table at the bottom.
  - Two colors (blue and orange), white background, and the handle repeated as a watermark in the middle.
- **Your version:** "Data Engineer vs AI Engineer", "Batch vs Streaming: how I'd answer it in an interview", "Data Warehouse vs Lakehouse". Bottom panel: "What I'd say in the interview".

### 2) The staircase / levels / roadmap infographic
- **Example:** Brij Kishore Pandey, *The AI Agents Staircase*, **7,327 reactions** ([post](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7306637799196119040)). Alex Xu, *The Generative AI Learning Roadmap*, **7,924** ([post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/alexxubyte_systemdesign-coding-interviewtips-activity-7300194175021400066-SY3c)).
- **Anatomy:**
  - Huge title with one word in red.
  - Three color bands (green Basic, purple Intermediate, blue Advanced) with a staircase arrow.
  - Each step is a box: a bold name, one line of explanation, and 1-3 tool logos.
  - A small author badge (photo + handle) sits inside the graphic, so the image travels with the name attached when it's reposted.
- **Your version:** "The data engineering interview staircase: junior → mid → senior → staff (what each level must show)", "From data engineer to AI engineer in 5 steps".

### 3) The tall mind map / curriculum
- **Example:** Chandrasekar Srinivasan, *AI Skills and How To Master Them*, **31,313 reactions**, the single biggest post in the dataset ([post](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7351616833126285312)).
- **Anatomy:**
  - A very tall image (800x2438).
  - One root node splits into 6 colored branches (LLMs, RAG, Agentic AI, AI Agents, AI Engineer, ML Engineer), each splitting into "Skills" and "Tools".
  - Clean white background, thin colored lines, small text. It gets saved because people zoom and come back to it.
  - Hook: "I spent 3+ hours in the last 2 weeks putting together this no-nonsense curriculum".
- **Your version:** "Everything a data engineer gets asked in a 2026 big tech loop" as a mind map (SQL, modeling, pipelines, Spark, streaming, system design, behavioral, AI round), built from questions you've actually asked or been asked. In the feed, very tall images can show only partly until someone taps, so put the title at the top.

### 4) The quote card / tweet-style meme
- **Example:** Alex Wang, a black tweet-style card ("To replace programmers with AI, clients will have to accurately describe what they want. we're safe."), **12,268 reactions** ([post](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7241033721120743424)). Justin Welsh: every top post is a plain quote-card image, at **4,259-7,306 reactions**. Daniel Murray's marketer memes get the most reactions per follower among the growth sellers (15.7 per 1k) but almost no comments.
- **Anatomy:** square 1080x1080, one sentence in big type, high contrast, a small handle watermark.
- **Your version (at most 1 a week):** one true, sharp line from the hiring side, e.g. "The pipeline is green. The dashboard is wrong." Memes buy reach but convert to followers poorly, so pair them with your lane.

### 5) A real photo of you in a real moment
- **Examples:** Lara Acosta's top post, "I feel uncomfortable posting this. / This is me in 2022…", **6,967** with a personal photo. Saywhat's Q1 2026 analysis lists photos showing your face among the visuals that work.
- **Conflict:** van der Blom says to avoid *selfies*. The way through it: no posed selfie; use a **moment** instead.
- **Your version:** you at the whiteboard during a meetup; the room at the 511-person workshop; you on a Live mock interview; a photo with a student who got hired (with permission). Best on founder posts (Sunday) and Across the Table (Monday).

### 6) Proof screenshots
- **What sellers use:** analytics screenshots, client before/after, "3.4 crore impressions" agency proof posts (Unnati Bagga). Proof posts turn reach into follows and leads.
- **Your version:** a resume bullet before/after (anonymised), a recruiter reply (with permission), your meetup member count, event registrant numbers. Never fake, never cropped to mislead.

### 7) PDF carousel (document post)
- **Evidence:**
  - Sam Browne's carousel took him from 15,000 to 27,000 followers in 24 hours (11,407 reactions, 2022).
  - A Matt Barker client at about 18k followers gained **about 2,000 followers from one carousel** in 2026 (700+ reactions, 100k impressions).
  - But carousels have the lowest per-follower score in the last 12 months of verified seller posts.
  - van der Blom: under 10k, posting carousels daily is "the wrong game"; documents are best for light, scannable content.
  - **Documents can't be boosted.**
- **Use it for:** walkthrough answers ("Design a pipeline for 1 billion events a day", stage by stage) and before/after resume teardowns.
- **Specs:** 1080x1350 (4:5), **8-12 slides**. Slide 1 shows the end result; one point per slide; consistent design; your face and handle on slide 1. The final slide should be one line pointing to Featured or the newsletter, with no ask to comment.

### 8) Video and GIFs (use sparingly)
- **Video:**
  - Per-post video reach fell 36% year on year.
  - Videos over 3 minutes get 1.21x reach when they hold attention.
  - Long written captions (20+ sentences) help: 1.13x, against 0.73x for under 5 sentences.
  - LinkedIn filters videos unrelated to the post text.
  - Lara Acosta calls video "overrated" for growth.
  - **Use it for:** 60-180 second clips from your weekly Live mock interview, with captions burned in (LinkedIn doesn't auto-caption uploads) and a full written post as the caption.
- **GIFs:** no reach data exists for GIFs in posts, and LinkedIn generally needs them converted to MP4. Animated architecture diagrams (ByteByteGo style) behave like **short videos**. Treat them as optional and test one; your Claude Motion pipeline in `stackle-video/` can produce them.

## 3. Your visual system (make it once, reuse it daily)

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Default size | 1080x1350 portrait (4:5). Square 1080x1080 only for quote cards |
| Header | Your face (circle) + "Chris · Stackle" + LinkedIn handle, top-left, on every infographic and slide 1. Rakesh and Brij both do this, so reposts carry your name |
| Colors | 2 brand colors + black/white. One accent color for the key word in the title |
| Type | One bold sans for titles, one regular for body. Minimum ~28px body at 1080 width so it reads on a phone |
| Density | Infographics can be dense (people zoom and save), but every panel needs a heading |
| Watermark | Small handle bottom-right (or mid-image, like Rakesh) |
| Never | Stock photos, generic AI-generated people/robots, logos you aren't allowed to use, numbers you can't source |

**Which visual for which day:**

| Day | Format | Visual |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Across the Table | Real photo of you or a recreated scorecard |
| Tue | Money & offers | One big number + source line (Levels.fyi), or a 2-number comparison |
| Wed | Inside the Interview | Clean redrawn diagram of your answer, or a card with the exact question |
| Thu | Before & After | Two-column before/after image, or an 8-10 slide carousel walkthrough |
| Fri | What Big Tech / AI labs expect | Cheat-sheet infographic (type 1 or 2) |
| Sat | Newsletter | Simple cover card with the question |
| Sun | Founder | Real moment photo |

**Tools you already have connected:** Canva (brand kit and templates), Excalidraw (hand-drawn architecture diagrams), and your `stackle-video` motion pipeline for short animated clips.
