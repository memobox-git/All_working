## 3. First niche: business brokers

### Why brokers
Planning OBIE picked brokers because they make $50k or more in commission per deal, most aren't technical, and they spend a lot of time on buyer emails, NDAs and follow-ups. Some AI tools exist for brokers, but nobody offers a whole AI team.

### What a broker can do in Version 1 (keep the pitch to this)
- Paste in a buyer inquiry, and the Sales agent drafts the reply plus a qualifying question.
- Ask the agent to draft the NDA chaser and the second and third follow-ups.
- Get a weekly update to the seller, drafted from a voice note.
- Get a first draft of a listing teaser or blind profile from the deal facts.
- Approve everything in "Needs you" and copy it into their own email.

**Product gap:** the Version 1 catalog is the "Courses and Coaching" pack. Brokers can get a custom stack through OBIE, but a Business Brokerage pack (Buyer Inquiry, NDA Follow-up, Seller Updates, Listing Teaser) would make onboarding take one tap instead of a conversation. That's a request for Building OBIE. It's listed in Chris's actions at the end.

### Where to get the list
Start with 100 brokers, built by hand. Don't scrape, and stay within each site's terms of use.

1. **BizBuySell broker directory.** Search by state. Each profile shows active listings, which gives you the personalization line for the email. Start with brokers who have 3 to 15 active listings: busy enough to feel the pain, but small enough to be a one-person shop.
2. **IBBA "Find a Business Broker".** Members are more established, and the CBI credential tells you they take the job seriously.
3. **State associations.** Use the member directories of the California Association of Business Brokers, Texas Association of Business Brokers, Business Brokers of Florida, and others. [VERIFY that each directory is public]
4. **LinkedIn.** Search the title "Business Broker" or "Business Intermediary", then filter to firms of 1 to 10 people. Use this to find each person's email and to warm them up with a profile view.

**Columns for each row:** name, firm, state, email, number of active listings, one listing to mention, LinkedIn URL, source, date contacted, status.

### Sending rules
- Send from a separate OBIE domain, not stackle.io, so Stackle's email reputation is protected.
- Write and send by hand from Chris's inbox, 15 to 20 a day. No bulk tools in month one.
- Every email includes Chris's postal address and a one-line opt-out. This is required for US commercial email under CAN-SPAM.
- No links in the first email, because links hurt delivery to the inbox.

**Post 5: One task, then gone**
```
Why my AI agents get one task and then disappear.

In OBIE, each department has a main agent. When I give it a goal, it breaks the goal into small tasks. Each task goes to what I call a nano agent. It gets only the context it needs, does that one job and is gone.

Two reasons:

Cost
→ a small job on a small model costs a fraction of a long conversation

Quality
→ an agent that only sees one task doesn't drift

Every task shows its credit cost before it runs, so I know what a goal will cost before I approve it.

I'd rather pay for ten small jobs than one long, confused one.
```
