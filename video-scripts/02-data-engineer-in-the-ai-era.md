**0:15–0:27 · WHAT THE JOB IS**
VO: "A data engineer builds the systems that move data from where it's created to where it's used. It has to arrive on time, it has to be correct, and it has to stay that way every day."
B-ROLL: an app event, then a warehouse table, then a dashboard.

**0:27–0:45 · WHAT AI CHANGED**
VO: "In dbt Labs' 2026 survey, 72% of respondents prioritize AI-assisted coding. But 71% worry about wrong data reaching stakeholders, and the share prioritizing data trust jumped from 66% to 83%. Code gets written faster, and the cost of getting it wrong has gone up."
ON SCREEN: 72% prioritize AI-assisted coding · 71% worry about wrong data reaching stakeholders · Trust priority 66% → 83%
SOURCE (lower third): dbt Labs, 2026 survey

**0:45–0:58 · WHAT JOB POSTINGS ASK FOR**
VO: "We looked at 6,877 job postings from May 2026. Pipelines came up in 74% of them, SQL and Python in 71% each, AWS in 44%, data quality in 43%, and data modeling in 38%."
ON SCREEN (bar chart): Pipelines 74 · SQL 71 · Python 71 · AWS 44 · Data quality 43 · Data modeling 38
SOURCE: InterviewStack, 6,877 postings, May 2026

**0:58–1:18 · WHAT TO BE PREPARED FOR**
VO: "So here's what to prepare for. First, you'll read far more code than you write, and a lot of it you won't have written. Second, you need to know what the right answer looks like before you run the query. Third, you test, so a mistake stops in the pipeline and never reaches a dashboard. And fourth, you explain your trade-offs out loud, because that is the interview. The candidates who stood out to me weren't the fastest typists. They could tell me why."
ON SCREEN (one per beat): 1 Read code you didn't write · 2 Know what correct looks like · 3 Test before it ships · 4 Explain your trade-offs
