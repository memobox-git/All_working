# Tools and costs

Price bands for planning. Prices change often and vary by country, so treat every figure here as approximate. When a price changes the plan's total, check it on the vendor's site (search the web if you can) and write the date next to it. If the person gave a currency, convert and say the conversion is approximate.

Bands used below: **Free**, **$** = under $20 a month or under $100 once, **$$** = $20-60 a month or $100-400 once, **$$$** = more than that.

## Choosing a method

| Method | Best for | Cost | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Live action (phone) | Presenters, products, real places, trust | Free-$ | Audio and light quality |
| Screen recording | Software, code, tutorials | Free | Blurry text, private info on screen |
| Motion graphics | Concepts, numbers, diagrams, brand moments | Free-$$ (time is the cost) | Takes longer than expected |
| Stock footage | Places, crowds, aerials, generic b-roll | Free-$$ | Looks generic; license limits |
| AI image + motion (parallax, slow push) | Illustrations, impossible scenes, mood | $ | Style drift between shots |
| AI video | Short b-roll, abstract or fantasy scenes, backgrounds | $-$$$ (credits) | Inconsistent characters and products, warped text, hands, straight lines; short clips |
| AI avatar / presenter | Training videos, translations, many versions | $$ | Uncanny feel; disclosure; trust |
| 2D animation | Characters, stories, explainer worlds | $$ (or a freelancer) | Skill and time |
| 3D | Product renders, technical visualisation | $$-$$$ | Skill and render time |
| Photo with movement (Ken Burns) | History, archives, real events | Free | Static feel if overused |

Rule of thumb: film real things for real; use AI and stock for what can't be filmed; use graphics for what can't be seen.

## Camera, audio, light and grip

| Item | Lean | Standard | Premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camera | Phone the person owns (shoot 4K) | Same phone, Blackmagic Camera app (free) for manual control | Mirrorless camera rental ($30-80/day) or purchase ($$$) |
| Microphone | Phone near the mouth; a second phone as voice recorder | Wired lav ($15-30) | Wireless lav kit, e.g. DJI Mic or Rode Wireless series ($100-300) |
| Light | Window light, white board bounce | LED panel or softbox kit ($40-120) | Bi-color COB light with softbox ($150-400) |
| Support | Books, phone clamp | Tripod with phone mount ($20-60) | Fluid-head tripod, slider ($60-200) |
| Stabilizer | Phone stabilization, careful walking | Phone gimbal ($80-150) | Camera gimbal ($300+) |
| Overhead rig | Shelf or a broom across two chairs | Boom arm / overhead mount ($20-60) | C-stand with arm ($100-200) |
| Teleprompter | Free prompter apps on a second device | Phone/tablet prompter glass ($40-120) | Full prompter ($200+) |
| Backdrop | A tidy room corner with a lamp | Paper or fabric backdrop ($30-80) | Rented studio |
| Drone | Stock aerials instead | Small drone ($300-500) plus registration and local rules | Licensed drone operator ($$$/day) |

Renting gear for a single shoot usually costs 5-15% of the purchase price per day.

## AI video, image, voice and music

These tools change monthly. Before relying on one for a key shot, check its current limits (clip length, resolution, commercial use, credits per clip) and plan for 3-10 generations per usable clip.

| Need | Tools to consider | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Text- or image-to-video | Google Veo (Gemini / Flow), OpenAI Sora, Runway, Kling, Luma Dream Machine, Pika, Hailuo | $-$$ monthly; top tiers $$$; credits run out fast |
| Images, style frames, storyboards | Midjourney, GPT image models, Gemini image models, Ideogram (good with text), Adobe Firefly, Flux | Free tiers to $$ |
| Voice-over (text to speech, voice cloning) | ElevenLabs, OpenAI voices, Descript voices | Free tier; $-$$ |
| Talking avatars | HeyGen, Synthesia, Captions | $$ / month |
| Music | Suno, Udio (check the plan's commercial licence) | Free tier; $ |
| Voice clean-up | Adobe Podcast Enhance, Descript Studio Sound, Resolve Voice Isolation | Free-$ |
| Background removal / rotoscoping | CapCut, Runway, Resolve Magic Mask, Canva | Free-$$ |
| Upscaling / frame interpolation | Topaz Video, Resolve Super Scale | $$ |

What AI video still does badly, so plan around it: the same face or product across many shots, readable text and logos, hands and fingers, fast or complex motion, straight lines in architecture, exact product details, clips longer than a few seconds. What it does well: short atmospheric b-roll, abstract and fantasy visuals, establishing shots of generic places, stylised scenes, and motion added to a still image.

## Captions and repurposing

- Free: YouTube auto captions (review them), CapCut auto captions, Resolve subtitles.
- Paid ($-$$): Descript, Submagic, Captions, Opus Clip (turns long videos into shorts).
- Burn captions into short-form and LinkedIn videos; most people watch with sound off.

## People and places

- **Presenter, actor, voice actor, editor, animator**: rates vary hugely by country, experience and usage rights. Give a range from local or marketplace (Fiverr, Upwork) listings and say "get 2-3 quotes".
- **Locations**: homes, offices and public spaces are free but may need permission; commercial shoots in public spaces often need a permit. Hourly location rentals (e.g. Peerspace) are $$ per hour in many cities.
- **Releases**: anyone recognisable on camera in a commercial video should sign a model release; private property needs a location release.

## Licensing and rules

Put these in the risks table when they apply:
- Music that isn't licensed for the platform gets the video muted, claimed or blocked.
- Stock and AI output licences differ on commercial use, resale and trademarks.
- Platforms require disclosure of realistic AI-generated or altered content (YouTube, TikTok and Meta all have labels).
- AI voice cloning needs the voice owner's consent.
- Drones need registration and airspace checks in most countries; many cities ban them.
- Don't show other brands' logos prominently in ads without permission.

## Cost-saving moves

- Use owned gear for the Lean tier and price the upgrade per item, so the person can buy only what changes the result most. Audio first, then light, then everything else.
- Time subscriptions to the edit week, use one month, then cancel.
- Rent gear for one-off shots instead of buying it.
- Batch shoots: film several videos' talking heads in one session with the same light.
- Build a reusable graphics template once and reuse it in every video.
- Push-ins, punch-ins and fake camera moves in the edit replace most motion gear.
