# Shot language, pacing and platform specs

Use this when choosing shots, moves and rhythm. Each entry says what the choice communicates and how to get it cheaply, because a director picks shots for their effect, not their name.

## Shot sizes

| Size | Frames | Use it to | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| EWS / establishing | A place, the person tiny | Set location and scale | Stock and aerials often fill this |
| WS / wide | Whole body plus surroundings | Show action and context | Phone 0.5x lens works |
| FS / full | Head to toe | Show body language, outfits, movement | |
| MS / medium | Waist up | Neutral talking, demos with hands | Default for presenters who gesture |
| MCU / medium close-up | Chest up | Talking head, trust, connection | The YouTube default |
| CU / close-up | Face | Emotion, emphasis | Punch in from 4K for a free "second camera" |
| ECU / extreme close-up | Eyes, a detail | Tension, texture, product detail | Macro lens or phone macro mode |
| Insert | Hands, object, screen | Show the thing being talked about | The cheapest way to add visual variety |
| OTS / over the shoulder | Foreground shoulder, subject behind | Conversations, "looking at a screen" | |
| POV | What the subject sees | Put the viewer in the action | Phone held at eye level, chest mount |
| Two-shot | Two subjects | Relationship, interview | Or one person plus a graphic |
| Overhead / top-down | Desk from above | Cooking, unboxing, drawing, crafts | Boom arm, or a phone on a shelf |

## Angles

- **Eye level**: neutral, honest. The default.
- **High angle** (camera looks down): the subject feels smaller or more vulnerable.
- **Low angle** (camera looks up): power, confidence, scale. Good for product hero shots.
- **Dutch / tilted**: unease. Use rarely.
- **Top-down**: clarity for hands-on work and flat lays.

## Camera movement

Movement should mean something: a push-in adds emphasis, a pull-out reveals, a track follows. Most moves can be faked cheaply.

| Move | Effect | Cheap way |
|---|---|---|
| Static / locked-off | Calm, credible | Tripod. Free with books and a phone clamp. |
| Push in / dolly in | Emphasis, "this matters" | Shoot 4K, edit on a 1080p timeline, scale 100% to 110-120%. Free. |
| Pull out / reveal | Context, ending | The same trick in reverse, or walk backwards on a gimbal |
| Pan | Follow or survey horizontally | Tripod head; slow and steady |
| Tilt | Reveal height (building, person) | Tripod head |
| Track / truck | Move alongside the subject | Phone on a towel slid along a table; skateboard; slider ($40-100) |
| Crane / jib / rise | Grandeur, reveal over an object | Lift the phone slowly from behind a desk edge; a selfie stick |
| Orbit | Hero moment around a subject or product | Gimbal; or rotate the product on a lazy Susan with a static camera |
| Handheld | Energy, documentary realism | Phone with stabilization; hold with both elbows tucked |
| Gimbal follow | Smooth movement with a moving subject | Phone gimbal ($80-150) |
| Whip pan | Fast, energetic transition | Pan fast between two shots and cut in the blur; add motion blur in the edit |
| Rack focus | Shift attention between depths | Phone portrait/cinematic mode; tap-to-focus while recording |
| Drone / aerial | Scale, location | Stock footage, a high window or rooftop, a 3D render of the area, or Google Earth Studio for non-commercial map flyovers (check its terms). Real drones need registration and airspace checks. |

## Lens feel

- **Wide (16-24mm, phone 0.5x)**: space, energy, distortion near the edges. Keep faces away from the edges.
- **Normal (28-50mm, phone 1x)**: natural. Talking heads.
- **Tele (70mm+, phone 2-5x)**: compressed background, flattering faces, isolates the subject. Needs more distance.

## Composition

- **Rule of thirds**: eyes on the top third line; subject on a vertical third.
- **Lead room**: leave space in the direction the subject looks or moves.
- **Headroom**: a little above the head. Less in close-ups.
- **Text space**: if text or graphics will appear, frame the subject on one third and leave the other side clean.
- **Depth**: foreground, subject, background. A plant or lamp in the foreground and a practical light behind lifts a cheap set.
- **180-degree rule**: in conversations, keep the camera on one side of the line between the two people so eyelines match.
- **Eyeline**: to camera means "I'm talking to you"; off camera means interview or story.

## Light

- **Key light**: the main light, about 45 degrees to the side. A window is a free, excellent key.
- **Fill**: softens shadows. A white foam board or a wall bounces the key for free.
- **Back / rim light**: separates the subject from the background. A lamp behind and to the side.
- **Practicals**: lamps in shot for warmth and depth.
- Never put a bright window behind the subject unless a silhouette is the goal.
- Turn off mixed light sources (warm bulbs plus daylight) or commit to one color.
- Filming into a building's windows from outside: shoot at blue hour (10-30 minutes after sunset), when the interior lights are brighter than the sky and the glass stops acting as a mirror.

## Transitions

- **Cut**: the default. Most transitions should be cuts.
- **J-cut / L-cut**: the sound of the next shot starts early, or the sound of the last shot carries over. It makes edits feel smooth and professional.
- **Match cut**: matching shape or motion across two shots. A memorable transition at no cost.
- **Whip / swipe**: energetic, for short-form.
- **Dissolve**: passing time. Use sparingly.
- **Graphic wipe**: branded transition between sections.

## Pacing by format

| Format | Typical shot length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shorts / Reels / TikTok | 1-3 s | Hook in the first 1-2 s, visually and in words. Captions on. Loop the ending into the start if you can. |
| Ads (6/15/30/60 s) | 1-4 s | Product or payoff on screen in the first 1-2 s, brand by second 3. One message. A clear call to action at the end. |
| YouTube explainer / education | A new visual every 4-8 s | Promise within 30 s. Change visuals on each idea, not on a timer. |
| Talking head / vlog | Punch-ins every 10-20 s | Alternate MCU and CU from one 4K camera. |
| Product demo | 3-6 s | Show the result first, then how. |
| Documentary / brand film | 4-10 s | Let moments breathe; sound design carries emotion. |
| Course / tutorial | Longer holds on screens | Zoom into the area being discussed; don't move the screen while the viewer reads. |
| Music video | On the beat or half-beat | Cut list follows the song structure. |

## Platform specs

| Platform | Aspect | Resolution | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube | 16:9 | 1920×1080 or 3840×2160 | Thumbnail 1280×720. Chapters help retention. |
| YouTube Shorts | 9:16 | 1080×1920 | Up to 3 minutes. Keep text out of the bottom 20% and right edge. |
| Instagram Reels | 9:16 | 1080×1920 | The profile grid shows a 3:4 crop of the center, so keep the cover image's subject there. |
| TikTok | 9:16 | 1080×1920 | Bottom and right UI zones are covered by captions and buttons. |
| Instagram / Facebook feed | 4:5 or 1:1 | 1080×1350 / 1080×1080 | |
| LinkedIn | 16:9, 1:1 or 4:5 | | Most people watch with sound off, so burn in captions. |

Speaking pace: about 130-160 words a minute. 150 is a safe planning figure, so a 60-second script is about 150 words.

## Sound

- Sound quality matters more than picture quality. Viewers forgive soft footage, not echo.
- Voice: mic within 15-30 cm (a lav or a close shotgun). Record 10 seconds of room tone.
- Music: duck it under the voice (around -20 to -30 dB below the voice, or roughly -25 to -30 LUFS for the bed).
- Loudness: about -14 LUFS integrated for YouTube and social.
- Sound effects: a whoosh on graphic moves, a click on UI, ambient sound on b-roll. They make cheap visuals feel finished.
