# Problem solving: when a shot or plan isn't working

Read this in fix mode, and when rating feasibility while planning. The goal is to keep what the shot is *for* and change how it gets made.

## Contents
- The method
- Scoring options
- Common blockers and go-to fixes
- How to write the fix reply

## The method

1. **State the shot's job in one sentence.** Examples: "Show this candle is handmade", "Establish that we're in a big city before we go inside", "Prove the SQL query removes duplicates". If you can't say it, the shot may not need to exist.
2. **Find the real blocker.** Common ones: money, gear, skill, time, permission or law, a tool's limits, physics or location, talent (no one to appear on camera, or someone who freezes on camera). Problems often hide another problem. "AI keeps warping the buildings" is a tool limit; "it looks cheap" usually means light, audio or pacing.
3. **Look at what the person already has that others don't.** An architect has 3D models of the building, a baker has a kitchen, a software company has its own product, a shop has customers who might film themselves. The best fix often turns one of these into the shot.
4. **Walk down the ladder** and find at least one option on each of the first three rungs:
   1. **Same shot, other means**: rent, borrow, a different tool, a different setting in the same tool, fake it in the edit.
   2. **Same idea, different medium**: live to motion graphics, AI video to AI still plus parallax, real aerial to stock or map flyover, actor to hands-only or POV, real set to tabletop miniature.
   3. **Same job, different idea**: a new visual metaphor that does the same job and is easy to get.
   4. **Restructure**: carry the information in voice-over, on-screen text, or a neighbouring shot.
   5. **Cut it**: if the job is already done elsewhere, removing the shot makes the video better.
5. **Score the options** (below), recommend one, keep one backup.
6. **Check the ripple**: does the change affect timing, voice-over, continuity with neighbouring shots, budget or schedule? Update them.

## Scoring options

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Fidelity (1-5) | How much of the original intent survives? 5 means the viewer gets the same thing. |
| Cost | What does it add to the budget? |
| Effort | Hours to get it, including learning time. |
| Risk | How likely is it to fail too? |
| Fit | Does it match the look of the shots around it? |

Prefer the option with the best fidelity for acceptable cost and risk. A 4/5 option that will definitely work beats a 5/5 option that might not.

## Common blockers and go-to fixes

**Aerial or drone shot not possible** (no drone, restricted airspace, no budget)
- Licensed stock aerial of a similar place; add a slow push-in in the edit.
- Google Earth Studio flyover for map-style or city-scale moves. Free and needs on-screen attribution, but Google's terms restrict commercial and advertising use of its imagery, so check the current terms before using it in an ad or brand film.
- Shoot from a high window, rooftop or bridge; tilt or push in.
- AI image of the skyline plus a 2.5D parallax move, which keeps buildings straight because the image never re-renders.
- A time-lapse from a high floor.

**AI video: buildings warp, straight lines bend, objects melt**
- Generate a clean still first, then image-to-video with slow, simple camera motion and a short clip (2-4 s).
- Ask for less motion: "slow push in, static subject, locked camera".
- Choose a model known for physical consistency, and use first and last keyframes if the tool supports them.
- Replace with stock or 2.5D parallax on a still.

**AI video: the character or product changes between shots**
- Film the real product; use AI only for the background or environment, then composite.
- Use image-to-video from the same reference image for every shot; keep shots short; cut on motion.
- Reduce the number of AI shots that show the face or product; use details, silhouettes or backs.
- For people, use a real presenter, hands-only or an avatar tool built for consistency.

**AI images or video: text, logos and labels are garbled**
- Never ask the model for text. Add text and logos in the edit, tracked onto the surface if needed.
- Use a text-capable image model for stills only.

**Hands look wrong**
- Frame hands out, use gloves or motion blur, or film real hands as an insert.

**Talking head feels boring**
- Punch-ins from 4K (alternate MCU and CU every 10-20 s).
- An insert, screen or graphic every 5-10 s, on the idea, not on a timer.
- A second angle from a second phone at 30-45 degrees.
- On-screen keywords; better light (a back light and a practical lamp); a more energetic delivery read from a script in short lines.

**Audio is echoey or noisy**
- Move the mic closer (lav or phone near the mouth), add soft furnishings, or record voice-over in a wardrobe full of clothes.
- Clean it with AI voice tools (Adobe Podcast Enhance, Descript Studio Sound, Resolve Voice Isolation).
- Re-record the voice-over and cut to b-roll where lips are visible.

**It looks cheap**
- Check light first: a window at 45 degrees, the bright background removed, a lamp behind.
- Then color: consistent white balance, a gentle contrast curve, one look applied to everything.
- Then audio, pacing (cut the dead air), and typography (one font, consistent sizes and positions).

**No one wants to be on camera**
- Voice-over with b-roll and graphics; hands-only demos; POV; screen recording; kinetic typography.
- An AI avatar, with disclosure and the accepted risk of an uncanny feel.

**No location or set**
- A clean corner, a plain wall with a practical lamp, a paper backdrop, or shallow depth of field with a tele lens.
- Green screen only with even light and a few metres of space; otherwise use AI background removal.
- Rent a space by the hour for one hero shot.

**No lights**
- Face the window. Shoot at the time of day when it's soft. Bounce with a white board. Diffuse a desk lamp with baking paper (keep it away from the bulb).

**Screen recording is blurry or hard to read**
- Record at 4K or 2x scaling, raise font sizes, zoom in during the edit on the area being discussed, slow the cursor down.

**Motion graphics are beyond the person's skill or time**
- Templates (Canva, CapCut); fewer and simpler graphics that build step by step; reuse one diagram with highlights instead of new graphics; code-driven templates if someone can code; a freelancer for just the hero graphic.

**Music copyright claim or no music licence**
- YouTube Audio Library, a subscription library with the channel registered, or AI music on a plan that allows commercial use.

**Not enough time**
- Cut shot count; batch film; reuse templates; record voice-over first and cut picture to it; drop the riskiest shot to its fallback now rather than on the last day.

**Over budget**
- Move items down a tier, rent instead of buy, use one-month subscriptions, replace a paid shot with its free fallback, and keep money for audio.

## How to write the fix reply

1. One line: the shot's job and the real blocker.
2. The recommendation and why it wins (fidelity, cost, effort).
3. Why the original approach failed, in plain words, so the person can avoid it elsewhere.
4. The other options, one line each with cost and fidelity.
5. What else changes (timing, voice-over, budget).
6. The updated board, with the change in the revision log.
